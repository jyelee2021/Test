Is This AI-Assisted Research Ethical?

A plain-language checklist for lawyers, judges, magistrates, CASAs, GALs, case managers, clinicians and other professionals who read or rely on research that used AI.

How to use this

  1. Have the study, report or summary in front of you. If you can, ask the researcher too.
  2. Answer each question. If you can't tell from what you have, choose Not sure. That is useful information.
  3. At the bottom, you'll get a short list of questions to ask before you rely on the research.
Yes = clearly shown No = missing or a problem Not sure = can't tell N/A = doesn't apply

Nothing you enter leaves your device. Answers are saved only in this browser.

Questions to ask before you rely on this research

    This is a screening tool, not a verdict. It helps you spot what to ask. It cannot prove research is sound or unsound, and it is not legal, clinical or professional advice. If the stakes are high (a court decision, a removal, a treatment plan), get a qualified expert to review the research.

    Based on the ETHICS framework: Lee, J. Y., Pace, G. T., Cha, H., Rao, S., Hahm, H. C., An, R., & Denby-Brinson, R. (2025). ETHICS: An ethical framework for artificial intelligence (AI) use in social work research. Journal of the Society for Social Work and Research, 16(4), 699–711. https://doi.org/10.1086/739116. The framework was written for social work researchers. This checklist adapts its six principles for professionals who read and use AI-assisted research.
